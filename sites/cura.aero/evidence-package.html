Escalations & Litigation

Data in. Filed package out.

When a passenger escalates to the authority or to court, this is every source that goes into the file, who supplies it, and what happens next. Every source is fetched over an API — the only thing anyone at the airline does is approve the response for sending.

What goes in
Every source, pulled in parallel
Weather, NOTAMs, slot messages, delay codes, booking data — every source fetched over an API the moment the request lands. Gaps are flagged, never left out quietly.
What you do
Approve it. That's the whole job.
The agent categorises the cause, attributes the delay, answers every item in the authority's structure and recommends defend or settle. Nobody collects, chases or writes anything. The handler reads it and presses send.
What comes back
Every ruling makes the next one better
Every ruling is stored with its reasoning and fed back into the drafting agent. Next quarter's package is built on what the authority actually decided this one.
Stage one

Intake and collection

A request arrives with a reply deadline. Cura reads it and matches it to the claim — or opens one, if the passenger never claimed with the airline and went straight to the authority. Then every evidence source is pulled at once over an API. Nothing here is a request to a person, and nothing waits on one; the connections below are set up once, at onboarding.

In practice

What they ask for, and what goes back

A real request lists its items and expects each one answered. The package mirrors that list item for item, so the case handler on the other side can tick their way down it.

From

To
claims@airline.example
Date

Dear Sir or Madam,

We have received a complaint concerning the diversion and subsequent delay of the above flight. In order to properly progress with this matter, we kindly request that you provide the following documentation:

  1. All relevant slot messages, including the original CTOT, any revised CTOTs, and related NOTAMs
  2. All NOTAMs valid at the relevant time for the route, departure airport, destination airport, and alternate/diversion airport
  3. All relevant Eurocontrol messages, communications, or network management notifications that influenced the CTOT allocation, delay, or diversion decision
  4. Any other operational messages, weather reports, or internal communications that may be material to understanding the cause and handling of the diversion and delay

Please note that a failure to reply within the period stated above may lead us to determine the matter on the basis of the information already before us.

Yours faithfully,
Case Officer, Passenger Rights Unit

Reference

Legal response engine — data & flow list

The complete step-by-step data and flow list behind the engine above: every trigger, source, computation and human checkpoint, end to end.

1

Request arrives → workflow trigger

workflows
  • Inbound email to a monitored address (NEB, airline legal, court)
  • Full message + attachments stored raw before any processing
  • Trigger fires one journey run per message
2

Read and understand the request

workflows, AI agent
  • Extract: booking reference, passenger names, flight number and date, claim type, what the authority is asking for
  • Read attachments too (passenger's complaint, authority's form, prior correspondence)
  • Confidence score per extracted field — anything below threshold goes to a human before a record is touched
3

Match to a claim in Cura

Cura database
  • Exists → load everything: claim record, airline's original decision and decision letter, evidence sealed at decision time, correspondence history
  • Doesn't exist → create the claim from the extracted PNR and flight, flagged escalation-first (evidence will have to be assembled retroactively)
  • Match confirmed by a person if the PNR or passenger name is uncertain
4

Classify the request and start the clock

workflows, AI agent + rules
  • Requester type: NEB (AESA, LBA, DGAC…) vs. airline's own lawyer vs. court
  • Authority file reference → idempotency key (reminders and duplicates don't create a second case)
  • Deadline rule from a jurisdiction table in Cura (AESA = 20 working days), reminders at 25/50/75%
  • Exactly what was asked for — itemised, kept verbatim in the case
5

Flight & rotation facts

Cura pulls — FlightAware AeroAPI
  • STD/ATD/STA/ATA, gate and runway times, actual routing, holds, diversions, alternate used
  • Inbound aircraft rotation (previous 2 legs, same tail) — for chain-of-delay defences
  • Aircraft registration, type, operating carrier
6

Weather

Cura pulls — public METAR/TAF/SPECI archive
  • Observations for departure, arrival and alternate, window = ETD −3h to ATA +1h
  • Published approach minima for the runway in use (AIP / Jeppesen)
  • Computed: did conditions sit below minima, and for how long
7

Airspace and ATFM

mixed
  • NOTAMs for all airports involved — reliable only from the airline's crew briefing pack months later [Airline API]
  • ATFM regulation / flow restriction, CTOT and slot messages [Eurocontrol — airline's operator credentials, approval-gated]
  • Strike and closure notices [Public]
8

Airline operational data

live API — day one
  • PNR, ticket numbers, passengers, fare class, booking date and channel
  • Check-in and boarding acceptance record, door-opening time
  • IATA delay codes (primary + sub), OCC/duty manager notes, crew duty limits if relevant
  • Maintenance record if technical cause claimed (AOG log, defect, MEL reference)
9

Passenger-side evidence

Cura + Plan3
  • Every notification sent: channel, timestamp, content, delivery receipt
  • Rebooking offered vs. accepted, alternative transport including ground
  • Duty of care provided: meals, vouchers, hotel, transfer
  • Claim history: what was filed, what was decided, decision letter, correspondence
10

Compute the facts — deterministic, versioned code, never AI

  • Arrival delay at destination in minutes, great-circle distance, EU261 compensation band
  • Notice period for cancellations (14-day rule), rerouting thresholds
  • Output = signed facts object, with source + timestamp + checksum per field
11

Gap check → tell the agent what's missing

  • Each requested item marked have / partial / missing
  • Missing items surface in the Cura agent view as a short task list: who supplies it, by when
  • Deadline recalculated against the gap; case flagged red if a mandatory item is unobtainable
12

Fight-or-pay recommendation

AI scores, rules guard
  • Inputs: facts object, cause category, evidence completeness score, claim value, historical outcomes for this cause + this authority
  • Hard rules override the AI both ways — technical fault with no extraordinary-circumstance basis → pay; evidence below threshold → pay or request extension
  • Output: recommendation, confidence, the 3 facts driving it, estimated exposure (compensation + care + authority cost)
13

Assemble the response

AI drafts prose only, from computed facts
  • Cover letter in the authority's language, matching their itemised request
  • Statement of facts, legal position with case law references, annex index
  • Annexes as PDFs with a per-document provenance page
14

Agent review in Cura

the only human step
  • Read the compiled file, edit any field or paragraph, override the recommendation with a mandatory reason
  • Every edit stored as a diff against the AI draft — this is training data
  • Approve → locked version, named approver, timestamp
15

Send and record

workflows sends, Cura stores
  • Submission to the authority's portal or by email, receipt stored
  • Status → Filed, pack version archived immutable
16

Follow-ups before judgment

  • Passenger's hearing response (10 working days in Spain) → new inbound event, re-enters step 2
  • Authority's additional questions → same path, incremental pack version
17

Judgment received

inbound → AI parse → structured record
  • Outcome: upheld / partially upheld / rejected, amount ordered, reasoning text
  • Which of our arguments were accepted and which dismissed — extracted as tagged reasons
  • Compliance branch: pay via Cura settlement, or appeal → new case linked to the parent
18

The learning store

Cura database — the asset
  • One outcome record per case: cause category, authority, country, evidence items present, our recommendation, the agent's decision, actual outcome, argument-level accept/reject
  • Feeds three things: the scoring model in step 12, the evidence checklist in step 11, the draft templates in step 13
  • Aggregate view per authority: win rate by cause, time to decision, arguments that NEB never accepts
  • Recalibration is a scheduled batch job with a versioned model — never silent, always reviewable
