Do You Know Your Airline's ImPax Percentage?

Passengers that experience cancellations, or medium and major delays without a quick resolution, produce almost 100% of an airline’s negative media that leads to customer churn.

This small percentage of passengers is an airline’s ImPax Percentage.

An airline’s ImPax Percentage is calculated as:


On average, an airline’s ImPax Percentage accounts for less than 1 in 13 flights (< 8%).

The Lower an Airline’s ImPax %, the Better.

The aim of this report is to help airlines focus their recovery operations by better understanding their ImPax Percentage in the context of:

  • Passenger estimates
  • Competitor airlines
  • Region, Tier, and Class
  • Sentiment analysis of monthly reviews

Global Disruptions Overview

This month's report analyses the disruption data of 276 airlines totalling 581,491 disrupted flights, across 2,699,544 planned flights, globally.

Global airline disruption was driven by several high-impact events, including the worldwide grounding of Airbus A320 aircraft, which forced thousands of cancellations, and widespread operational issues across major European carriers. India experienced severe turbulence as IndiGo cancelled more than 1,200 flights, while volcanic-ash restrictions added further strain. In the United States, the federal government shutdown triggered FAA-mandated 10% flight cuts, contributing to more than 9,500 cancellations and extensive delays. Although global flight volumes fell by around 10% and disrupted flights declined nearly as much, disrupted passenger numbers increased slightly, showing that large airlines bore responsibility for disproportionately high-impact disruptions across fewer but fuller flights.

This is a comparative view of the average ImPax Percentage of airlines by Region and Tier, and the average Total Percentage of disruptions, which includes minor delays.

Region

Asia Pacific

In November 2025, APAC air travel was hit hard: the urgent global recall of Airbus A320 jets triggered widespread cancellations — from ~90 flights at Jetstar to mass delays at major carriers — while airlines scrambled to implement safety updates. In India, IndiGo's heavy dependence on A320s made it especially vulnerable, resulting in airports packed with delayed or cancelled domestic flights. Even though overall passenger volumes in the region dropped by about 6%, almost every delay and cancellation metric rose, disrupting an estimated 1.2 million more passengers than in October. The disruptions disproportionally affected high-capacity carriers reliant on affected fleets.
