Do You Know Your Airline's ImPax Percentage?

Passengers that experience cancellations, or medium and major delays without a quick resolution, produce almost 100% of an airline’s negative media that leads to customer churn.

This small percentage of passengers is an airline’s ImPax Percentage.

An airline’s ImPax Percentage is calculated as:


On average, an airline's ImPax Percentage accounts for less than 1 in 13 flights (< 8%).

The Lower an Airline’s ImPax %, the Better.

The aim of this report is to help airlines focus their recovery operations by better understanding their ImPax Percentage in the context of:

  • Passenger estimates
  • Competitor airlines
  • Region, Tier, and Class
  • Sentiment analysis of monthly reviews

Global Disruptions Overview

This month’s report analyses the disruption data of 243 airlines totalling 802,658 disrupted flights, across 2,988,248 planned flights, globally.

August saw global aviation contend with multiple sources of disruption as peak summer demand placed continued pressure on networks. Severe weather drove significant disruption across Asia-Pacific, while Europe faced a combination of storms, volcanic activity and persistent ATC capacity and staffing constraints. Middle East instability continued to affect capacity, routings and operating conditions, with regional capacity remaining below 2025 levels. Despite these pressures, global airline capacity increased 2.6% year-on-year, highlighting the challenge of maintaining operational resilience as traffic volumes remain high.

Region

Asia Pacific

APAC experienced significant weather-driven disruption throughout August, with successive typhoons impacting major aviation markets across North Asia. Typhoon Dolphin caused widespread cancellations across Japan and Taiwan before hitting eastern China, where Shanghai's two airports cancelled around 1,300 flights in a single day, followed by more than 900 the next day. Later in the month, further severe weather disrupted Japan. Australia faced a different challenge, with air traffic control staffing constraints at Sydney causing widespread delays and knock-on disruption across the domestic network.
