Do You Know Your Airline's ImPax Percentage?

Passengers that experience cancellations, or medium and major delays without a quick resolution, produce almost 100% of an airline’s negative media that leads to customer churn.

This small percentage of passengers is an airline’s ImPax Percentage.

An airline’s ImPax Percentage is calculated as:


On average, an airline's ImPax Percentage accounts for less than 1 in 13 flights (< 8%).

The Lower an Airline’s ImPax %, the Better.

The aim of this report is to help airlines focus their recovery operations by better understanding their ImPax Percentage in the context of:

  • Passenger estimates
  • Competitor airlines
  • Region, Tier, and Class
  • Sentiment analysis of monthly reviews

Global Disruptions Overview

This month’s report analyses the disruption data of 248 airlines totalling 587,740 disrupted flights, across 2,766,769 planned flights, globally.

September saw global aviation move beyond the summer peak, with flight volumes easing by almost 10% as seasonal demand began to fall. Disruption remained widespread, however, driven by a varied mix of operational pressures. Europe faced significant ATC disruption, industrial action and volcanic activity, while severe weather impacted networks across North America and APAC. LATAM also experienced weather-related disruption, including significant thunderstorms affecting Mexico City operations. Typhoon activity caused further disruption across Asia, while geopolitical instability continued to affect the Middle East. Rather than one defining event, September was characterised by multiple, overlapping sources of disruption across the global network.

Region

Asia Pacific

Flight volumes across APAC fell almost 10% in September as the summer peak eased, potentially compounded by significant weather-related disruption across the region. Despite this, most delay metrics improved during the month. Super Typhoon Ragasa severely impacted Hong Kong operations, while volcanic activity caused widespread cancellations and airport closures in Indonesia. Monsoon rains, thunderstorms and flooding created further disruption across major Asian hubs, including Bangkok, while Australia experienced a combination of weather and ATC-related delays. Overall, September saw lower traffic and improved delay performance despite several significant operational events.
